# Player Expectations: What Sim Racing and Racing-Game Players Value, Recommend or Abandon (2024-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-18. Scope: product priorities for GridCorsa (GT3 sim-lite, invented cars, real-named circuits, PC/Android/browser, 12-driver online rooms). Caveat that applies throughout: OverTake.gg and Boxthislap pages returned HTTP 403 or bot-check walls to the fetch tool, so several OverTake and Boxthislap figures below come from search-result snippets and article titles, not full-page reads. Where that is the case it is marked "(snippet)".

## 1. What sim racers rank as most important (surveys and polls)

### Takeaway
No rigorous industry survey exists. Community polls (OverTake, r/simracing, a Steam questionnaire, Rennsport's own survey) consistently put **AI quality, career/progression, damage, and online competition** at the top of the wish lists, and show the audience is **GT-racing-first (about three in four name GT as a favourite discipline)**, increasingly online (about 70% prefer multiplayer in r/simracing's 2026 sample), on wheels, and growing more into VR and ultrawide screens. Force feedback and stability decide whether reviews are positive (see section 2) more than they show up as poll wishes, because players assume them as the baseline.

### Cited Findings
- OverTake.gg community poll on what to improve most in sim racing (1,146 responses, roughly early 2025 per the search tool's dating; snippet): career/progression 36.9%, damage about 27%, car customisation 17.9%, livery editors 13.8%, audio 9.2%, online competition 9.2% — [OverTake](https://www.overtake.gg/news/ai-career-damage-what-the-overtake-community-wants-to-improve-most-in-sim-racing.2884/)
- OverTake.gg poll on single-player career mode (roughly mid-2025; snippet): adaptive, human-like AI was the top request at 64.2%; tier-based progression (grassroots up to top championships) was second at 57.5% — [OverTake](https://www.overtake.gg/news/what-sim-racers-really-want-from-a-single-player-career-mode-experience.3481/)
- "Sim Racing Trends 2022 vs 2023 vs 2025" questionnaire analysis: GT racing (GT3/GT4/GTE) was the most popular favourite discipline in 2025 at 74.5%, open-wheel second at 41.1% (snippet) — [GTPlanet mirror](https://www.gtplanet.net/forum/threads/sim-racing-trends-2022-vs-2023-vs-2025-questionnaire-analysis.431948/); [OverTake thread](https://www.overtake.gg/threads/sim-racing-trends-2022-vs-2023-vs-2025-questionnaire-analysis.283905/)
- The same questionnaire series, posted on Steam as a "2025 edition": 85.7% PC as the primary platform; ACC played by 45.5% and iRacing by 31.1%; 70.1% use only wheel force feedback (no extra haptics); SimHub is the most-used companion app at 39.2%. User-run survey; indicative only — [Steam Community, Sim Racing Questionnaire 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1551360/discussions/0/597398346071577303/)
- r/simracing survey analysis (community-built dashboard; 2,364 responses in total: 2022 n=619, 2023 n=715, 2025 n=615, 2026 n=415):
  - Sims played in 2026: Le Mans Ultimate 44.7%, iRacing 34.5%, ACC 23.9% (ACC down from 45.8% in 2022). Note: the page also shows LMU at 28.1% in 2022, before LMU existed, so that baseline is suspect (it may be a renamed rFactor 2 category).
  - Displays in 2026 (multi-select): single monitor 33.3% (69.6% in 2022), ultrawide 31.6% (0.3% in 2022), VR headset 28.2% (about 24.5% in 2022, derived). No triple-screen figure given.
  - Mode preference in 2026: ranked/competitive 32.3%, casual multiplayer 25.5%, league racing 12.5%, single player 28.0% (down 12.5 points from 2025); overall multiplayer preference 70.4% (56.4% in 2025).
  - Wheelbases in 2026: Moza 24.1%, Fanatec 21.7%, Logitech 17.9% (Logitech about 38.5% in 2022, derived).
  - Spending: 59.5% have setups costing $2k or more (29.4% in 2022); only 7.5% under $500 (23.1% in 2022).
  - Average age about 35.1. The page reports no stated values or frustrations.
  — [r/simracing Survey Analysis](https://v0-simracingresults.vercel.app/)
- Rennsport's community survey (June 2026, 423 respondents, voluntary): top priorities were AI behaviour, steering-wheel compatibility, console stability; audio fourth; larger race grids fifth. The developer put overall performance ahead of adding content — [Boxthislap](https://boxthislap.org/rennsport-prioritizes-ai-force-feedback-stability-next-development-phase/); [Simulation Daily](https://simulationdaily.com/news/rennsport-improvements-first-community-survey/)
- iRacing's Safety Rating is a no-blame incident-points system. It decides licence grade, which decides which series you can enter, and it groups drivers of similar ratings. A separate Elo-style iRating measures skill. Both change only in official ranked races — [iRacing support](https://support.iracing.com/support/solutions/articles/31000156960-iracing-how-to-safety-rating); [iRacing 101: Ranked vs Unranked](https://www.iracing.com/?p=274256); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRacing)

### Inferences
- The surveyed hardcore segment (r/simracing, OverTake) is wheel-owning, spends a lot on hardware, and moves fast to whatever sim has the best online racing (LMU's rise, ACC's fall). It is not a pad or mobile audience. GridCorsa's GT3 focus matches the single most popular discipline.
- Online ranked racing with a clean-racing system (SR/licences) is now the main reason a hardcore player stays. GridCorsa already has SR, licences and ranking, which is the right foundation. What players will judge is whether matchmaking separates clean drivers from wreckers in practice.
- AI quality ranks first in both the career poll and Rennsport's survey. For a small game with sparse lobbies, bots fill the gaps, so AI quality matters even more than it does for a big sim.
- VR (about 28%) and ultrawide (about 32%) are now mainstream in the hardcore segment. "No VR" will be a visible gap for wheel users, though not for pad or mobile players.

### Gaps
- No Traxion player survey for 2024-2026 was found. Traxion covers news and esports, not surveys.
- No survey asked players to rank FFB, physics, netcode, laser-scanned tracks, price and performance against each other. Those rankings have to be inferred from review drivers (section 2).
- No Boxthislap reader poll could be read (bot-check wall).
- No triple-screen adoption figure for 2025-2026 was found.

## 2. Steam reviews of recent launches: what drove positive vs negative reviews and refunds

### Takeaway
Across AC EVO, Le Mans Ultimate, Rennsport, Project Motor Racing and Forza Motorsport, negative reviews cluster round the same things: **performance on ordinary PCs, weak or unpredictable AI, thin single-player content, broken promises or cut features, unstable online play, monetisation seen as greedy, and force feedback that feels wrong or absent**. Positive reviews nearly always name **handling/physics and FFB feel** first, then **good online racing**. Players forgive a lot in Early Access if the driving feels right. They forgive almost nothing if the driving feels wrong, the game runs badly on their PC, or promised features disappear.

### Cited Findings
**Assetto Corsa EVO (Early Access from 16 January 2025)**
- Steam rating is "Mixed". Snapshots range from 54% positive (9,084 English reviews) to about 59% positive (about 17,000-19,000 reviews) — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3058630/Assetto_Corsa_EVO/); [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/3058630/Assetto_Corsa_EVO/reviews); [Eneba](https://www.eneba.com/hub/free-games/how-to-get-assetto-corsa-evo-free/)
- Launch complaints: poor optimisation, VR hard to play at launch (later reported fixed), barely working AI, buggy multiplayer, no free-roam. Praise: the car selection with different specs and trims, handling and force feedback, and the argument that "it's Early Access" — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/assetto-corsa-evo-steam-vr-release-user-reviews/); [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3058630/discussions/0/590687864233186097/)
- At launch, a hidden online requirement blocked the career mode — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/assetto-corsa-evo-draws-crowds-despite-rough-early-access-launch/z577be)
- In early February 2026 Kunos dropped the planned XP, currency and economy career in favour of a reworked Driving Academy. Update 0.5 (12 February 2026) disabled Career Mode. Gamereactor reported part of the community was "very angry", and Polish press headlined that "players feel cheated" — [Traxion](https://traxion.gg/assetto-corsa-evo-ditches-original-career-mode-concept/); [Top Gear](https://www.topgear.com/car-news/gaming/assetto-corsa-evo-ditches-its-original-career-mode-plans-a-reworked-driving-academy); [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/assetto-corsa-evo-rolls-out-update-05-amid-career-mode-controversy-1674123/); [PPE.pl](https://www.ppe.pl/news/399175/gracze-czuja-sie-oszukani-tworcy-popularnych-wyscigow-wycofali-sie-z-obiecywanej-funkcji.html)
- The original Assetto Corsa (2014) has about twelve times as many Steam players as EVO (Boxthislap headline). It broke its own concurrent-player record nearly 10 years after release (Traxion headline) — [Boxthislap](https://boxthislap.org/assetto-corsa-more-players-than-evo/); [Traxion](https://traxion.gg/assetto-corsa-breaks-concurrent-player-records-nearly-10-years-after-release/)

**Le Mans Ultimate (Studio 397 / Motorsport Games)**
- About 79% positive across about 14,500 Steam reviews ("Mostly Positive"). Critics and players praise the online racing as competing with the best of the genre. They criticise a barebones single player (custom race weekends only), aggressive DLC monetisation, bugs, optimisation and online stability. Base price $39.99 — [Vaporlens summary](https://vaporlens.app/app/2399420/le_mans_ultimate); [Top Gear review](https://www.topgear.com/car-news/gaming/le-mans-ultimate-review-does-endurance-racing-sim-have-staying-power); [Metacritic](https://legacy.prod.metacritic.com/game/pc/le-mans-ultimate/critic-reviews); [Wasdland](https://www.wasdland.com/game/le-mans-ultimate-2399420)
- Players say online racing in every class works with the base content and free cars, which softens the DLC complaint (anecdotal) — [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2399420/discussions/0/604147301143806694)
- Despite these complaints, LMU became the most-played sim among r/simracing respondents in 2026 (44.7%) — [r/simracing Survey Analysis](https://v0-simracingresults.vercel.app/)

**Rennsport (Competition Company)**
- Steam "Mixed" at about 43%. On one day SteamDB showed only 27 players in 24 hours. OpenCritic puts it in the 10th percentile — [Traxion](https://traxion.gg/rennsport-makes-official-statement-to-address-community-concerns/); [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/19665/rennsport)
- Review drivers: few cars and tracks for the price, paid liveries, unpredictable AI ("suicidally reckless" or "unfathomably slow"), bugs, low frame rates, slow menus. Critics did credit a "solid racing sim at the heart" — [Top Gear review](https://www.topgear.com/car-news/gaming/rennsport-review-light-content-and-plagued-bugs); [Metacritic PS5](https://legacy.prod.metacritic.com/game/playstation-5/rennsport/critic-reviews); [PC Games (DE)](https://www.pcgames.de/Spiele-Thema-239104/News/Rennsport-Steam-Kritik-1486719/)

**Project Motor Racing (Straight4 Studios, released 25 November 2025)**
- Launched "Very Negative", with players openly refunding. Later "Mostly Negative" at 28-37% positive. Complaints: handling, force feedback ("weak, non-existent or highly unrealistic" for many; others praised its detail after tuning settings), CPU bottlenecks, weak AI, poor performance on lower-spec PCs (admitted by the studio), "unfinished, overpriced" — [Operation Sports](https://www.operationsports.com/project-motor-racing-launches-to-criticism-as-developers-outline-fixes/); [Gamer Matters](https://gamermatters.com/project-motor-racing-is-out-now-but-not-without-issues-devs-respond/); [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/299970/project_motor_racing); [iXBT Games, April 2026](https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/04/24/411119-project-motor-racing-padaet-37-polozhitelnykh-otzyvov-i-ischeznuvshee-dlc.html)
- The studio cut staff on 8 December 2025. Its roadmap promised physics, FFB and online-stability fixes plus triple screens and VR. Recent reviews turned "mostly positive" after the 2.0 update while the overall score stayed negative — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Motor_Racing); [Traxion roadmap](https://traxion.gg/project-motor-racing-not-backing-away-from-challenges-fix-roadmap-expected/); [Traxion 2.0](https://traxion.gg/project-motor-racings-2-0-update-long-road-to-redemption/); [Top Gear review](https://www.topgear.com/car-news/gaming/project-motor-racing-review-its-current-state-its-a-bit-a-mess)

**Forza Motorsport (Turn 10, October 2023; older data)**
- Steam peak under 5,000 concurrent players at launch, 44% positive. All-time Steam peak 4,619 — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/10/11/forza-motorsport-launch-peak-ccu-steam-mixed-reviews); [Tracker.gg](https://tracker.gg/population/steam/2440510)
- Turn 10 named its "top three" complaints "rides, rules and racers": car progression (each car had to be levelled to unlock parts), penalties and race rules, and AI — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/turn-10-promises-big-changes-to-address-top-three-forza-motorsport-complaints-rides-rules-and-racers); [GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/?p=131103); [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/turn-10-addresses-complaints-about-forza-motorsport-progression-ai-and-race-regulations)
- Update 6 made all parts available at Car Level 1 — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/forza-motorsport-upate-6-fixes-progression/)

### Inferences
- The recurring negative drivers translate into a launch checklist for GridCorsa: (1) runs well on modest PCs and phones; (2) bots that neither dive-bomb nor crawl; (3) enough single-player structure (career and championships exist; keep them working at launch); (4) never announce a feature and then cut it; (5) pricing and monetisation that cannot read as greedy; (6) FFB that feels right out of the box on common wheels (Logitech, Moza, Fanatec, Thrustmaster) with sensible defaults, because a new sim with bad FFB is hard to rescue (PMR).
- A grind that gates basic parts or upgrades behind per-car levels (Forza Motorsport) drew strong backlash even from casual players. GridCorsa's progression should unlock cosmetics or cars, not performance parts.
- PMR and Rennsport show that a late fix lifts recent reviews but rarely the overall score or the player count. First impressions on Steam are close to permanent for a small title, so a soft launch (demo, playtest, Early Access with a clearly stated scope) is safer than a big-bang release.
- Fewer cars and tracks is tolerated when the price fits (the original AC is cheap and still dominant). Rennsport was punished for charging full price for little content.

### Gaps
- No studio has published refund rates. Refunds are anecdotal for every title.
- No independent topic-coded Steam review dataset was found for these titles. Vaporlens and Raijin are automated summaries.
- iRacing and ACC were not re-checked here (outside the 2025 launch window).

## 3. Casual and mobile racing players: what they value and what frustrates them

### Takeaway
Evidence for mobile racing players is thin and mostly from vendor review-mining blogs. In it, **pay-to-win, intrusive ads, bugs and grind** are the top reasons for negative reviews, while **visuals, customisation and controller support** are praised. Players accept rewarded (opt-in) ads but quit over forced, disruptive ones. Controller support and offline play are what "best mobile racer" lists recommend for core players (GRID Autosport is the usual pick).

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 review-mining roundup: aggressive monetisation and pay-to-win account for 34% of negative mobile-game reviews overall. For sports and racing games, bugs are the top complaint (31%), then monetisation (28%) and realism (16%) — [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/gaming-app-reviews-what-mobile-gamers-hate-most-2026)
- Review analysis of a mobile racer (Pocket Champs, 150 reviews): 35% of negative reviews criticise pay-to-win and 28% complain about excessive ads — [Glotier](https://glotier.com/games/pocket-champs)
- Review analysis: players rarely object to ads as such. They object to how ads are placed and how often — [AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/blog/what-mobile-game-players-want-monetization-insights-from-app-store-reviews)
- 2019 US survey (older): 76% of US mobile gamers prefer opt-in rewarded ads, 24% prefer mandatory ads. Data.ai survey of about 4,000 players: only 6% would rather pay for the game than see ads. AppHarbr survey: 58% quit immediately because of disruptive ads, 84% uninstall after repeated exposure — figures as reported in [AppFollow monetisation guide](https://appfollow.io/blog/mobile-game-monetization); [Axios 2022](https://www.axios.com/2022/05/25/mobile-games-playable-ads-report)
- CarX Street (mobile and PC): the grind for cash is the most consistent complaint. Graphics and customisation are praised. Touch-steering problems after an update and physics oddities were also reported. Install size about 1.8-1.9 GB on iPhone. About 65% audience score (1,762 reviews) — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1114150/car_x_street); [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/17394/-/reviews); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id1458863319?l=en-gb); [Justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1458863319/carx-street/reviews); [Gamebrain](https://gamebrain.co/game/carx-street)
- GRID Autosport is the most-cited "premium, offline, controller support out of the box" Android racer. Asphalt Legends is praised for MFi, Xbox and PlayStation pad support (low-quality listicle sources) — [Plarium](https://plarium.com/en/blog/best-racing-games-for-android/); [Recharge](https://www.recharge.com/blog/en-ca/ca/best-android-games-with-controller-support-2026); [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/mobile-games/5-best-controller-supported-games-apple-iphone)
- Casual console and PC players in Forza Motorsport mostly did not touch online racing (about 90% had never finished an online race), which suggests casual players live in single player and quick races — [RacingGames.gg](https://racinggames.gg/article/most-forza-motorsport-players-havent-completed-a-single-online-race)

### Inferences
- For GridCorsa on Android and the browser: no pay-to-win (cosmetic-only or car-access purchases), no forced interstitial ads, small first download with circuits fetched on demand, stability on mid-range phones, and pad support on Android. Coins for real money (per the backlog) must be clearly cosmetic to avoid the top negative-review driver.
- Casual and mobile sessions should be short: a quick race or a time trial that fits in under 10 minutes, with one-tap rematch. The planned time-trial mode fits this well.
- The browser version is a low-friction trial path no competitor sim offers. A playable instant demo removes the purchase risk that drives refunds.

### Gaps
- No mobile racing-specific player survey (session length, touch-control preference) from 2024-2026 was found.
- No data on how large an install is acceptable for mobile racing players.
- The status of Real Racing 3 (EA) in 2025-2026 was not verified.

## 4. Real car licences vs good invented cars

### Takeaway
There is no hard evidence that missing licences hurt sales. Several commercially successful racers use only invented or "tribute" cars (Wreckfest, BeamNG.drive, Art of Rally, Split/Second). Invented cars bring a concrete benefit: **unrestricted damage and destruction**, which manufacturers otherwise limit. The risk is specific to the hardcore GT3 sim segment, which judges authenticity, so invented cars need believable, recognisable archetypes and strong customisation (liveries) to stand in for brand attachment.

### Cited Findings
- Manufacturers control how licensed cars may be damaged and must approve damage models. Games often scale all cars to the strictest brand ("playing to the weakest link"). Race cars get more latitude than road cars. Small teams skip licensing because the approval loops cost staff time. Invented brands usually still reference real ones so they read as familiar (article dated 18 September 2026) — [RacingGames.gg](https://racinggames.gg/article/the-real-reason-racing-games-ditch-licensed-cars-for-fictional-brands)
- Same article: Split/Second (all invented cars) sold about 86,000 in one NPD month against about 31,000 for Blur (50+ licensed cars; only five days on shelves). The indie game Over the Hill (invented vehicles) passed 200,000 demo downloads and peaked at 5,954 concurrent players (June 2026). The article itself says this does not isolate licensing as the cause — [RacingGames.gg](https://racinggames.gg/article/the-real-reason-racing-games-ditch-licensed-cars-for-fictional-brands)
- Wreckfest has no licensed content, only obvious tributes. It sold over $1 million in a single week in Early Access and drew generally positive reviews. Steam Workshop mods add real-car looks — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreckfest); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/228380/discussions/0/4033598880929600641/); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/wreckfest-is-murder-disguised-as-car-racing-1827126590)
- BeamNG.drive (all invented brands such as Gavril, Ibishu and ETK) is about 97% positive on Steam with about 350,000-400,000 reviews and averaged about 20,000 concurrent players in mid-2026, with an all-time peak of about 38,000-39,000 (December 2025). Sales estimates range from 8.6M to 24M (unreliable third-party estimates). It ranked second in US PlayStation Store sales for October 2026 behind Call of Duty — [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/an-obscure-physics-sim-is-reeling-in-almost-as-many-players-as-forza-horizon-6-on-steam); [Steambase](https://steambase.io/games/beamngdrive); [Traxion](https://traxion.gg/?p=109776)
- Player opinion is split: some say invented cars "add some originality"; poll threads show players wanting both. BeamNG players note invented cars can be crashed to pieces, which licensed ones cannot — [BeamNG forum](https://www.beamng.com/posts/955562/); [GTPlanet poll](https://www.gtplanet.net/forum/threads/fictional-or-authentic-race-cars-poll.331527); [OverTake, "Would you race fictional cars and tracks?"](https://overtake.gg/news/comments/47649) (could not be fetched)

### Inferences
- GridCorsa's invented cars plus real circuit names is a defensible mix. Real circuits give the authenticity hook (sim racers practise Spa and the Nürburgring for leagues) while invented cars free up full damage with parts flying off, a feature licensed GT3 sims are restricted on.
- To make invented cars stick: give each one a clear archetype and character (front-engine V8 brute, mid-engine screamer, and so on), believable GT3-style balance of performance, a name worth remembering, and deep paint/number/rim customisation so players attach to "their" car. Wreckfest and BeamNG show that personality matters more than badges.
- For the hardcore wheel segment, physics credibility has to replace the authenticity a licence gives. Publishing how each car is modelled (a real GT3 archetype's power, weight, aero class) helps.

### Gaps
- No survey isolates how much licences matter to GT3 sim buyers. No data was found on early Rennsport, Gran Turismo's fictional cars, Trackmania or Inertial Drift.

## 5. Features that create shareable moments and word of mouth

### Takeaway
The strongest documented word-of-mouth engines are **crash and damage physics (BeamNG, Wreckfest)**, **modding and community content (Assetto Corsa)**, and **livery creation and sharing (LMU added custom liveries in June 2025; Rennsport was criticised for paid liveries)**. Livery editors are a stated priority for about 14% and car customisation for about 18% in OverTake's poll. Specific evidence on replays, photo mode, telemetry and broadcast tools as word-of-mouth drivers was not found, though they are standard in the sims that keep leagues.

### Cited Findings
- BeamNG.drive's destruction-physics sandbox draws concurrent player numbers close to Forza Horizon 6 on Steam — [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/an-obscure-physics-sim-is-reeling-in-almost-as-many-players-as-forza-horizon-6-on-steam)
- Assetto Corsa's longevity is credited to its modding community (Custom Shaders Patch, Sol/Pure visuals, Content Manager), physics, low price and modest hardware needs. About 11,000 active players and a 24-hour peak of about 14,000 in a recent snapshot; all-time peak about 20,200 (December 2025). The franchise passed 20 million players — [Tracker.gg](https://tracker.gg/population/steam/244210); [SimRacingSetup](https://simracingsetup.com/assetto-corsa/best-assetto-corsa-mods/); [Traxion](https://traxion.gg/assetto-corsa-franchise-surpasses-20-million-players/); [Boxthislap](https://boxthislap.org/assetto-corsa-still-king-of-the-track/)
- AC EVO responded by going "all-in on modding" (Boxthislap headline). Commentators note that AC1's mods do not transfer to a new engine — [Boxthislap](https://boxthislap.org/assetto-corsa-evo-goes-all-in-on-modding/); [Boxthislap](https://boxthislap.org/will-assetto-corsa-evo-prove-divisive-in-its-own-community/)
- LMU launched custom liveries in June 2025: template-based, shown to all drivers and spectators in online events, but not carried over to non-pre-registered or hosted servers. ELMS ran a livery competition whose winner became the #26 car in the game. A "Livery Hub" for sharing launched later — [LMU](https://lemansultimate.com/creating-custom-liveries); [Race Control](https://news.racecontrol.gg/?p=1406); [Dakins Design](https://www.dakinsdesign.com/news/lmu-official-artwork)
- Rennsport's paid liveries were a named review negative — [OpenCritic reviews](https://opencritic.com/game/19665/-/reviews)
- EA WRC players requested more livery-editor features (mirroring, more patterns) — [EA forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/wrc-general-discussion-en/livery-editor---suggestion-thread/8386402/replies/8386425)
- Rennsport's 2026 roadmap added Le Mans content and modding support — [GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/rennsport-2026-roadmap-deluxe-edition-price-cut-le-mans-modding-and-revamped-ai/)

### Inferences
- GridCorsa's damage with parts flying off, together with replays (planned), is its most clip-friendly feature. A replay camera with a one-key "save last 30 s" clip export and a photo mode would turn crashes and close finishes into shareable video. Since licensed sims are restricted on damage, this sets GridCorsa apart.
- An in-game livery editor with sharing (download someone's design by code) costs little next to content and feeds social sharing. Showing liveries to every driver in a room matters (LMU's limit on hosted servers is a complaint point). Never sell liveries as paid items (Rennsport).
- Leagues spread a sim through word of mouth. Private rooms with set rules (fixed setups, fuel and tyre multipliers, qualifying format), results export and a spectator or broadcast camera are what league admins look for. Setup sharing (planned) helps too.
- Modding is the strongest proven longevity driver, but it is costly to support. A cheaper middle step is open liveries and track-day presets, then car or track mod support later.

### Gaps
- No data was found on replays, photo mode, telemetry export (SimHub at 39.2% usage shows telemetry tools are common) or broadcast tools as specific drivers of reviews or sharing.
- No YouTube or TikTok analytics for crash clips were found.

## 7. Content cadence expectations and modding as a longevity driver

### Takeaway
No hard data on expected cadence was found. The evidence points to two working models: **paid DLC packs tied to a real series (LMU)**, which keep players but draw "greedy" criticism, and **mods (AC1)**, which keep a 2014 game ahead of its 2025 successor by about twelve times. Rennsport's survey shows players rank **stability, AI and FFB above more content**. Players punish content cut more than they punish slow content.

### Cited Findings
- LMU sells content as season packs and track passes (ELMS Season Pass, US Track Pass, 2024 Packs). "Aggressive monetisation and DLC strategy" is a major point of contention in its reviews, yet its share of players grew to the top of r/simracing's 2026 sample — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2399420/le_mans_ultimate); [Gamegeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/dlc/le-mans-ultimate-elms-season-pass-steam-pc); [r/simracing Survey Analysis](https://v0-simracingresults.vercel.app/)
- The original Assetto Corsa: about 12x EVO's Steam player base; new player records nearly 10 years after release; longevity credited to mods and low hardware needs — [Boxthislap](https://boxthislap.org/assetto-corsa-more-players-than-evo/); [Traxion](https://traxion.gg/assetto-corsa-breaks-concurrent-player-records-nearly-10-years-after-release/); [SimRacingSetup](https://simracingsetup.com/assetto-corsa/best-assetto-corsa-mods/)
- Rennsport (2026) said it would put performance ahead of adding content. Its June 2026 survey ranked AI, wheel compatibility and stability above audio and grid size — [Boxthislap](https://boxthislap.org/rennsport-prioritizes-ai-force-feedback-stability-next-development-phase/)
- Project Motor Racing: DLC "vanished" amid falling reviews (April 2026 headline) — [iXBT Games](https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/04/24/411119-project-motor-racing-padaet-37-polozhitelnykh-otzyvov-i-ischeznuvshee-dlc.html)
- Forza Motorsport's thin single-player content left it dependent on online play that most players skipped. Its player count fell sharply within a year — [Forza forums](https://forums.forza.net/t/the-lack-of-online-players-is-very-concerning-and-not-enough-single-player-events/694026?page=4); [Tech4Gamers](https://tech4gamers.com/forza-motorsport-player-base-almost-dead)

### Inferences
- For a solo developer, a steady and predictable cadence (for example a new car or layout every 1-2 months, plus weekly rotating online events or time-trial challenges) is more achievable than large packs, and rotating events refresh play without new assets.
- The 12-driver room cap and a small player base mean lobbies may be thin. Scheduled official races at fixed times, which concentrate players, plus good bots to fill grids, are likely to matter more for retention than a bigger car count.
- Opening liveries first, then tracks and cars to mods, is the proven longevity lever (AC1). It is also the best answer to "only 3 tracks".

### Gaps
- No survey on expected cadence (how many cars or tracks per month or year) was found.
- No rFactor 2 retention data from 2024-2026 was found (an r/rfactor2 "resurrection" thread exists but was not read).
- No direct comparison of retention between paid-DLC and free-update models in sims.

## Segment summary (synthesis of the above; inference)
- **Hardcore wheel sim racers**: FFB feel and physics first (they decide the review), then clean ranked online racing with SR/licences, AI that races like humans, performance, wheel compatibility, VR/ultrawide/triples, and fair DLC. Most like GT3. They desert a game quickly if the driving feels off.
- **Casual pad players (PC and console)**: short single-player races and career progression without a grind, assists that make a pad feel good, visible damage, customisation. Most never race online.
- **Mobile players**: no pay-to-win, no forced ads, stability on mid-range phones, small download, touch and pad options, short sessions, cars to collect and customise.
- **Shared review-killers**: poor optimisation, bad AI, cut features or broken promises, greedy monetisation, thin content for the price.
